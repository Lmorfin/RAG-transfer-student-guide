As a person who graduated and transferred from community college, I get asked this question every so often. My general response is no, and I follow up with I regret going to community college first (I wrote off high school, I was bored and my teachers sucked). Assuming that you’re financially able to do so, here is my reasoning:
All in-state tuition at California public colleges is pretty cheap and there’s almost always some form of scholarship or other financial aid available. The biggest difference here is if you can live at home while attending community college (I did this).
Transferring to a state or UC school is a complex process and the way it is setup is a major disservice to students.
First, as others have mentioned, there’s no guarantee a school will accept you. Some community colleges have agreements in place with some nearby 4 year schools but it isn’t a given. Virtually all 4 years now are super impacted. There was a time a few years ago when many state schools were not accepting any transfer students particularly during the economic downturn.
You can’t just do your “General Ed” and then transfer. In order to transfer into a 4 year, you must transfer into a specific major. In order to transfer to a specific major, you must take undergrad classes for that major. Those classes are different at every college in the UC and State system. I.E., Cal State East Bay will have different undergrad requirements than Cal State Fresno for Computer Science. This little detail caused me a 1 semester delay in transferring. And if your CC doesn’t offer the classes needed to transfer, you’re SOL.
Even when you successfully transfer, a 4 year school can choose to not accept some classes as credit forcing you to retake the same classes. This is completely at the accepting college’s will. The only thing that can save you here is if you earn an associates degree (which may or may not align with your requirements to transfer). Then by law a 4 year has to give you undergrad credit but can still reject credit for additional courses. This was a problem with some of my Math classes when I transferred.
There’s a huge experience you miss by not starting at a 4 year that I didn’t realize until it was too late.
By living at home for a couple years after graduating high school, I really feel like I delayed “growing up.” Its something you can’t do until you live on your own. College is a pretty safe place for “growing up”, giving you a lot of leeway to make mistakes and do dumb things.
For everyone I was friends with in college, I was never quite as “close” with them as the people who they started college with. Living in the dorms and “growing up” together seemed to give people much stronger bonds and relationships than I ever established in college.
This, to me, I think was the single biggest thing I missed.

I have no idea why people are saying otherwise.
Reasons:
You'll save a significant amount in student loans. People answering your question by saying in-state tuition is “cheap" have no idea what they're talking about. For example, UC Berkeley in-state costs are just under $40,000 per year full time. The typical student takes 5 years to graduate, so that's a total loan burden of roughly $200,000. But if you transfer there for the last two years only, that loan burden is $80,000 instead.
In California, students transferring from a community college to a UC have a HIGHER acceptance rate than freshman applicants to a UC. Your chances increase significantly as a transfer student in good academic standing.
California community colleges have designed a whole matriculation process to make it easy to transfer to a UC or CSU. Thats one of their stated purposes in the CCCO charter.
California community colleges have guaranteed acceptance programs with the CSU system and are working on a UC program. This means students with good grades are guaranteed a transfer spot at a CSU.

The answer to your question (community college with goal of UC or straight to a state school) depends on your financial situation, your GPA, your major and your academic goals. A degree is like a passport. It will get you there, but it’s up to you to make something of the adventure that lies before you.
FINANCIAL SITUATION: If you can afford it, and the state school is a good one, take the opportunity at a state school now. Start your degree! Certainly a community college can save you money, but it isn’t always the best path. Robin Williams for example came from an affluent background and he first went to community college before attending Juilliard. In the end he didn’t finish Juilliard and was successful just the same. Everyone has a path in life and college won’t make or break you. It’s more about your determination, not your financial situation.
GPA: Have less than stellar grades? You can improve your GPA at a community college to help gain higher admittance at a UC as a transfer student and you’ll save money simultaneously,
MAJOR: Some majors are time sensitive (music and dance performance, for example), where you need to train as early as possible. Cal State Long Beach has a wonderful conservatory within the university and has generous full-ride scholarships, so you might not have to wait for UC Irvine or UCLA to get a successful start to your career.
ACADEMIC GOALS: If your path includes a master’s degree then you’ll want to get into the highest UC you can as an undergraduate to set the foundation for your graduate degree. Go to a junior college first so you can aim high. It’s much easier to gain admittance to a UC as a junior than as a freshman and much easier to get into a master’s program if your undergraduate degree is from a UC.
BEWARE that going to a community college can get you into your dream UC campus, but there is no such guarantee you will get into a top campus. The Transfer Application Guarantee doesn’t apply to all campuses.
You may like to know that employers take a holistic view of your application and a degree from SDSU or CalPoly San Luis Obispo is just as attractive to an employer as a degree from a UC San Diego or UC Irvine. An internship might be the tiebreaker, so look beyond the degree.
